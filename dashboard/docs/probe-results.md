## F1/F2 — kitty graphics: better than the plan assumed

Every capability query answered `OK`:

```
DA1  ESC[c                        -> ESC[?62;1;4c          (a `4`: sixel also claimed)
     ESC_Gi=31,s=1,v=1,a=q,t=d,f=24  -> ESC_Gi=31;OK ESC\  raw RGB
     ESC_Gi=32,s=1,v=1,a=q,t=d,f=32  -> ESC_Gi=32;OK ESC\  raw RGBA
     ESC_Gi=33,a=q,t=d,f=100         -> ESC_Gi=33;OK ESC\  PNG
     64x64 RGB in 4 chunks of 4096B  -> ESC_Gi=34;OK ESC\  chunked transmission
```

Three consequences for the design:

1. **`f=24`/`f=32` are accepted, so the PNG encode leaves the hot path.** The plan budgeted
   an `encode_png()` of unknown cost (2–8 ms guessed) on every board change because the
   Konsole MR's wording — "any format supported by `QImage::loadFromData`" — implied
   container sniffing and therefore PNG-only. It does not. Send raw RGB straight out of the
   `tiny_skia::Pixmap`: no deflate, no `png` crate on the render path. The cost is ~4/3 the
   escape bytes of PNG at this flatness, which at one board change per move is irrelevant.
2. **Chunking works**, so a 1144px board (~27 chunks at 4096 base64 bytes) is fine.
3. **The capability query is a positive answer, not an inference.** Keep it: the Lab Note
   ("do not assume a terminal cannot do something without asking it") cost this project its
   entire half-block renderer once, and the mirror of that mistake is equally available.

Visual, `a=T,t=d,f=24,q=2` placed by cursor move at row 3 col 3, screenshotted: crisp
magenta border, clean green diagonal, flat grey field. **No banding, no speckle, no palette
shift** — which is the whole point of leaving sixel behind. Nothing to quantise means nothing
to dither, which means nothing to shimmer between frames.

## F3 — sibling text does not damage the picture

Ten lines of text written at column 40 on rows 4..13, i.e. *on the image's own rows*, with
the image left completely intact.

This is the single largest difference from the sixel path and it retires a whole family of
Lab Notes. Under `rich` + sixel, changing one cell made `rich` rewrite the whole line, which
wrote unstyled padding over the picture and erased the board a row at a time — hence
"putting *anything* styled on a row the sixel image occupies", "drawing a sixel image under
any styled cell", and "putting anything that animates anywhere in the board column". Konsole
keeps kitty graphics in a layer separate from the text grid, so none of that applies.

What is left for `CellDiffOption::Skip` is the narrower job of stopping ratatui writing
*inside* the image rect. That still needs the M2 byte-recording-backend test, but the
terminal-level risk (R2) is gone.

## F1e — delete by image id works

`ESC_Ga=d,d=i,i=41,q=2` removed the placement cleanly and left the sibling text untouched.
So **`ESC[2J` is no longer the only eraser** (R3 dead). Placement ids can be reused and
retired explicitly, which is what makes a resize clean rather than a full-screen wipe.

## F6 — resvg replaces rsvg-convert

Same SVG string (generated by the installed python-chess, so this measures rasterisers and
not my port) into both paths at 1144px:

| case | max channel Δ | differing >2/255 | structural (no 3×3 match) | box4 max Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| empty | 41 | 0.264% | 0.128% | 10 |
| start | 108 | 1.958% | 1.046% | 16 |
| midgame | 108 | 1.304% | 0.713% | 15 |
| check+flip | 108 | 1.960% | 1.047% | 16 |

**The raw differing-pixel count is the wrong metric and it nearly produced a false negative.**
1.96% at max Δ 108 reads like a failure; it is entirely antialiasing gamma on piece outlines
and coordinate glyphs. Two things establish that: mean-downsampling both images 4× drops the
worst delta to 16/255 (AA averages away, structure does not), and the 8×-amplified diff image
(`f6-diff.png`) shows nothing but one-pixel outlines — no missing glyph, no shift, no
fill-rule error. Compare `f6-resvg.png` against `f6-rsvg.png` by eye and they are the same
board.

**The seam rule survives the change of rasteriser**, which was the other half of F6:

| size | mod 26 | resvg strays | rsvg strays |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1144 | 0 | **0** | **0** |
| 1152 | 8 | 4 | 6 |
| 1170 | 0 | **0** | **0** |
| 1180 | 10 | 6 | 6 |
| 1196 | 0 | **0** | **0** |

Zero stray pixels at every multiple of 26 and nonzero at every non-multiple, in both
rasterisers. `snap26` stays, and this table becomes an assertion in M2 rather than a comment.

Deleted from the runtime by this result: `rsvg-convert`, ImageMagick, libsixel (whose local
1.10.5 exits 0 while writing nothing), fontconfig and any font dependency — python-chess
draws its coordinate labels as `<path>` outlines, so `resvg` builds with
`default-features = false`.

## F7 — nvml and zbus replace the subprocesses

**nvml.** Everything the plan wanted, including the field the `machine` panel exists to show:

```
gpu util 99%   mem util 68%   12766/16303 MiB   77 C   280.9/300.0 W
fan 86%   SM 2752 MHz   mem 13801 MHz   pstate P1   throttle SW_POWER_CAP
per-process VRAM:  1345757 train.py  11160 MiB
                    591098 kwin_wayland  499 MiB
                   1194446 claude-desktop 107 MiB
```

`running_compute_processes()` returned `Used(_)` for every process — no `Unavailable`, so the
systemd sandbox does not block attribution. The panel can answer "is one starving the other"
directly instead of inferring it from one aggregate, which is the exact mistake CLAUDE.md
records as costing a whole session. Note the throttle reason is live right now:
`SW_POWER_CAP` at 280.9 of 300 W, which is a fact no current panel could show.

**zbus on the user bus.** `Connection::session()` works, and `ListUnitsByNames` fetched all
six units in one round trip:

| unit | load | active | sub |
|---|---|---|---|
| `chess-gpu-bot.service` | loaded | active | running |
| `chess-gpu-lab.service` | loaded | active | running |
| `chess-gpu-train.service` | **not-found** | inactive | dead |
| `chess-gpu-watchdog.timer` | loaded | active | waiting |
| `chess-gpu-train-watchdog.timer` | loaded | active | waiting |
| `chess-gpu-rating.timer` | loaded | active | waiting |

Two things this makes possible that the `systemctl show` path did not:

- **`not-found` is a distinct load state, not an error.** Today `chess-gpu-train` renders as a
  permanently red dot, which trains you to ignore the row. The new panel can say "not-found"
  and, more usefully, notice that `chess-gpu-train-watchdog.timer` is armed and pointed at it.
- **`NRestarts` is 0 for `chess-gpu-bot` even though the watchdog SIGKILLed and restarted it
  twice today.** Confirmed via D-Bus, not inferred. `watchdog.py` uses `systemctl kill` +
  `restart` rather than letting `Restart=always` fire, so the counter never moves and
  `machine_panel` suppresses the restart line entirely. The restart count must come from
  `~/.local/state/chess-gpu/watchdog.json` and the watchdog's journal, never from `NRestarts`.

Timer `NextElapseUSecMonotonic` values are in the 1.18e12 range against realtime timestamps
of 1.78e15 — the two clocks confirmed distinct, as the plan warned. Convert monotonic against
`CLOCK_MONOTONIC`, realtime against the epoch, and never mix them.

## F4 / F5 — outstanding, and why

Neither could run: the last engine record is 61 minutes old and the bot is in
`Seeking rapid game with opponent`, so there is no live game to measure.

**F5** (is `/api/account/playing` subject to the 3-move delay) needs a live game and ~20 plies
of polling. It is also worth noting that `scripts/watch.py` is running right now and already
polls that endpoint every 2 s, so the measurement must either reuse that process's data or
wait for it to stop — adding a second poller is the thing that put the bot into a 40-minute
no-challenge stall once.

**F4** (does a second `/api/bot/game/stream/{gameId}` coexist with lichess-bot's) needs a
**casual** game, and the bot is configured `challenge_mode: "rated"` with `challenge.modes:
[rated]`. Arranging a casual game means changing the config of a live rated bot, which is a
decision for the owner rather than something to do unattended. Until then F4 stays unrun and
`bot_stream` stays `false`, which costs nothing: the foundation is telemetry-primary and the
public stream is simply labelled `+3 moves`.
