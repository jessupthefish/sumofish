## Context

Steven asked for a full audit of SumoFish and a Council on direction, specifically whether to
scale the model up. He widened the size question mid-run: not fixed on 18M, any size, 36M in
scope. Cost and time are explicitly not constraints; the budgets are one RTX 5070 Ti and
attention.

The audit found the case against scaling does not survive (the width sweep measured the
gradient clip, not width; the capacity diagnostic discriminates nothing across a 1000x range;
Ruoss et al. 2024, this project's lineage, gets +234 Elo from 9M to 136M at 2000x our data).
It also found the measurement apparatus that would judge a bigger net is broken in enumerable
ways: 98 of 104 archived matches are fixed-simulation and structurally blind to per-node cost,
the trust tool trusts by default, checkpoints are hashed by path, 88 of 104 matches share 3
seeds, and no contamination index exists for the state-value bag that gated both promotions.

Council Round 2 then overturned Round 1's cost model and with it the width call. The bench
behind the "d=512 is cost-neutral" fit never varied the term it predicts:
`scripts/bench_search.py:111` loads the fixed 9M policy net once, before the loop, and only
the value model is rebuilt per arm, so the measured 3.06x is
`(tree + p256 + v1024) / (tree + p256 + v256)` and the entire d=256 policy pass, the thing
fusion deletes, is buried in the intercept. Every cost figure in this session is therefore
unfalsified. Cost is superlinear in width, at minimum d^1.18, and the corrected
decomposition (per-pass ≈ 46.7 + 2.700e-4·d² us at batch 64, 73% launch overhead at d=256)
shows the fusion prize is collapsing two launch-bound passes into one, a prize that is
**maximal at the smallest width and spent back by every increment of width**. Three seats
(Empiricist, Skeptic, Quartermaster) independently landed on d=384. Separately, the -168 Elo
that has kept `compile` (CUDA graphs, 1.9x on the dominant cost) disabled since 2026-07-30
turns out to rest on twenty games in a bundle with `dedup`, and compile cuts the same launch
term the fusion case is built on, so it must be measured before any capacity commitment.

Intended outcome: `compile` priced alone and deployed if it wins; a single fused two-head net
at d=384 (19.68M) accepted or rejected by an instrument that can see it; the live reliability
defect (33 no-move events at the deployed TC) fixed; and the ledger of false claims cleaned so
no future session re-inherits them.

## The call

**Fuse the two nets into one shared trunk with a value head and a policy head at d=384, L=8,
12 heads (head_dim 32), 19.68M params, trained with an alternating-batch loader, gated by an
early clock probe at 50k steps, and accepted on the equivalent-Stockfish-node criterion at the
deployed time control. Before committing any training hours, price `compile` alone at the
clock and re-run the corrected cost bench, because either can change the arithmetic.**

Where the Council split, the calls and the reasons:

- **d=384, not d=512.** Corrected ladder (provisional, pre-Arm-C, pre-compile): d=384 is
  0.67x today's per-node cost, +0.57 sims-doublings; d=512 is 0.91x, +0.13; d=512 costs 37%
  more training for 77% less search gain, so it is dominated. d=576 is below the acceptance
  instrument's ±5.3% resolution and is struck. The honest ordering is not "biggest that is
  free" but "largest gain the instrument can actually see", and that is d=384 (4x the
  resolution). Counterpoint recorded honestly: d=512 is the only rung with exact 2x
  function-preserving duplication; d=384's growth is approximate (partial duplication shifts
  LayerNorm statistics). Pick d=384 anyway: an unmeasurable win is not a win under this
  project's own philosophy, and the growth gate below handles the approximation.
- **Width, not depth.** Cost scales as d·L, params as d²·L; d=256/L=16 (17.3M) is dominated
  by wider-shallower at equal time. The post-LN depth objection was withdrawn and is wrong.
- **18M is rejected.** An unattested midpoint whose only recommendation is arithmetic
  tidiness. AZ (~27.6M) and Leela (27M-45M) license ~20-45M; 9M is the outlier.
- **Growth argued on option value, not convergence.** `9M-sv-long` hit 97% of final quality
  at 1% of the run, so warm-starting saves 10-20% of training at best. What it actually buys:
  every checkpoint deployable, so the ±14.7 clock match runs at step 50k (5.8 GPU-h in)
  instead of after 68 GPU-h, converting an all-or-nothing bet into a probe with an early
  abort worth ~60-90 GPU-h of avoided sunk cost.
- **Deployment currency is the wall clock / equivalent node budget.** Fixed-sims matches
  price per-node cost changes at exactly zero in both directions, and 98 of 104 archived
  results are fixed-sims. The acceptance criterion is defined in Phase 4 and it is legal
  under PHILOSOPHY's "what restores a number" paragraph.

with batch size (d384 costs 1.055x at batch 64 and 1.347x at batch 128), and `compile` cuts

Falsifier: disagreement beyond two intervals means every published number needs second-seed

## Phase 3: build and train the fused d=384 net. ~68 GPU-h, one early abort built in.

Prerequisites: 0.4, 0.6, 0.12, 0.13, 1.1, 1.4 GO decision, 1.6, 1.7.

**3.1 Model.** Two-head model in `sumofish/model.py`: shared trunk d=384, L=8, 12 heads
(head_dim 32), value head (64 HL-Gauss bins) and policy head (1968 moves) on the trunk
output. `sumofish/rust_mcts.py:112-125` collapses to one forward pass.

**3.2 Growth.** `scripts/grow_net.py`: partial Net2Net duplication from the deployed d=256
value net (duplicate 4 of 8 heads and a matching channel subset, halve duplicated fan-out;
positional buffer constructed consistent with the duplication map and pinned via the 0.12
config field). State plainly: **this is approximate.** Exact preservation exists only at 2x
(measured: full duplication 0.000e+00, zero-padding 1.6105 through LayerNorm; partial
duplication shifts LN statistics). The gate is therefore recovery, not identity: the grown
net's value-head clean held-out loss must return to within 0.014 of the donor's by step 10k
(~0.5 GPU-h; the 97%-at-1% training dynamic makes this cheap). Falsifier: no recovery by 10k
means the growth scheme is broken; fall back to cold start and accept the later first probe.
The policy head starts cold either way; per-head held-out logged from step 1.

**3.3 Growth test.** `tests/identity_grow.py` on the `tests/identity_*.py` precedent:
measure and record max|delta| of the grown value head vs donor on a batch of real positions
at init, and assert the step-10k recovery gate. Wire into `tests/run_all.sh`.

**3.4 Alternating-batch loader.** The bags are not positionally aligned (527,633,465 vs
530,310,443 records, independently shuffled, 0 of 10 sampled indices match), so one forward
pass cannot train both heads. Extend `train.py` and `sumofish/data.py`: alternate value steps
(SV bag) and policy steps (BC bag), trunk taking gradients from both. `train.py` is
single-target by construction (`--target` :95-98, `is_value` :361/:391); budget a day of desk
work plus a `tiny` smoke run showing both heads learning. This is ordinary multi-task
learning, not AlphaZero's aligned setup: the AZ/Leela precedent licenses the parameter count,
not the regime. Known failure mode: one objective dominating the shared trunk; per-head
curves are the tripwire, a `--policy-weight` knob is the fix.
**Host RAM, not VRAM, is the binding resource here.** Two loader instances means up to 20
DataLoader workers on a 31 GB box with 4 GB already in swap, and this machine has livelocked
for 18 hours on memory pressure before. Cap total workers (~6 per bag or a shared pool) and
watch `/proc/pressure/memory`. The loader does NOT double I/O (alternating draws ~210 KB/s
from one bag either way); the cost is concurrency and RSS. Do not let "two 36 GB streams"
become folklore. VRAM is likewise not a constraint: gradient accumulation is a measured 2.6%
tax, so drop VRAM from the design discussion entirely.

**3.5 Train.** 1.2M alternating steps at effective batch 1024, ~68.4 GPU-h (train exponent
1.587, anchored to the measured 42.8 h at 9M; confirm with the 1.2 probes). Warm-started
trunk, cold policy head. Run under systemd with `KillMode=mixed`, `TimeoutStopSec=300`,
retention policy from 0.13 on. Watch the effective-LR multiplier from 0.7: if the mean sits
far below the 9M's ~0.33, the run is clip-confounded like the sweep; adjust `--clip` or LR
until comparable and record the choice.

**3.6 The early probe, the point of the growth. 9.8 GPU-h, exclusive box.** At step 50k
(~5.8 GPU-h in), run fused vs today, `--time 0.5`, 600 games, idle box. The grown net is
deployable at every checkpoint, so this converts the 68-hour bet into a 6-hour-in probe.
Abort criterion, fixed now so the yardstick cannot move: if the probe is negative beyond its
interval, or if at 600k steps the value head's clean held-out is not better than the deployed
9M's by at least 0.014, stop and write it up. That is the scaling hypothesis failing
honestly, and it is a result.

## Phase 4: acceptance and deployment.

**4.1 Select on held-out loss** (clean subset, EMA weights, value head gating, policy head
reported). Never best-of-N on puzzle accuracy (±1.5% sigma; it already promoted the worse
checkpoint once).

**4.2 The acceptance criterion, in a currency the project trusts.** From PHILOSOPHY's own
"what restores a number" paragraph: **a capacity change is ACCEPTED iff, played at the
deployed time control against a Stockfish node ladder bisected to parity, its equivalent
Stockfish node budget rises, with the 95% lower bound above the incumbent's point
estimate.** One currency, one population, no Elo-per-doubling, no ruler transfer; running on
a clock means the wider net's per-node cost is already paid inside the measurement. Measured
resolution ±5.3% in equivalent nodes at ~3.8 GPU-h per accept/reject decision. d=384's
modelled cost-side gain alone (+21.5% equivalent nodes, pre-compile) is 4x that resolution;
recompute the expectation with the 1.1/1.4 numbers before running.

**4.3 Calibration gate.** Run `scripts/eval_heldout.py --calibration` on the candidate and
the 9M baseline over the same deterministic batches; a Brier/ECE regression alongside a match
win is the confidence-not-correctness failure mode. Use the HELD-OUT version, not the
match-archive one: see the 0.14 retraction, where 90% of the archive statistic turned out to
be the match result. `scripts/calibration.py`'s residual column is a coarse secondary screen
with a +-0.05 row null, not a gate. Stockfish adjudication in the harness stays.

**4.4 Continuity anchors. ~3.8 GPU-h.** Re-run the two chain-free vs-Stockfish anchors
(700n and 1600n, 400 sims, 1.88 h each) so the external ladder continues across the model
change. Fixed-sims head-to-head vs incumbent (2000 games, ~4 h, ±6.7) is an optional
judgement-per-node screen, never the gate.

**4.5 Deploy** via the extended `promote.py`. Both heads are now one file, which deletes the
two-net promotion split entirely (the policy net's untracked manual rollback ceases to
exist as a category). Previous nets kept as the rollback pair, provenance with content
hashes, rollback doc tracked. Re-measure the parity-ladder rungs when convenient; the old
rungs describe the old net. Retune (2.4) before the acceptance match, not after.
